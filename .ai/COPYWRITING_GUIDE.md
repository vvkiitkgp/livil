# Copywriting Guide

> This document defines how LiViL communicates.
>
> Every headline, caption, carousel, announcement, release note, landing page, and marketing asset should follow these principles.
>
> We are not trying to sound like a marketing team.
>
> We are documenting the journey of building a thoughtful product for people who love music.

---

# Our Voice

LiViL speaks like a founder.

Not like a corporation.

Not like a social media manager.

Not like an advertising agency.

The voice should feel:

- Calm
- Honest
- Curious
- Thoughtful
- Confident
- Human
- Minimal

Never loud.

Never desperate.

Never exaggerated.

---

# Our Personality

We are builders.

We obsess over details.

We appreciate good design.

We enjoy solving difficult problems.

We care deeply about music.

We believe products should speak for themselves.

---

# How We Write

Short sentences.

Simple words.

Clear ideas.

Leave space.

One idea at a time.

Don't try to sound intelligent.

Be easy to understand.

---

# Confidence

Confidence comes from showing.

Not claiming.

Bad

> The best music platform ever built.

Good

> We rebuilt shared playlists from scratch.

---

Bad

> Revolutionary listening experience.

Good

> Listening together should feel effortless.

---

# Show. Don't Sell.

Every post should demonstrate value instead of claiming value.

Instead of

> Fast.

Show

> Video of opening the app in under a second.

Instead of

> Beautiful.

Show

> A beautiful screen.

Instead of

> Powerful.

Explain the feature.

---

# Founder Voice

Write as someone building the product.

Examples

"We spent the last two weeks redesigning this."

"This feature took four iterations."

"We removed more code than we added."

"We weren't happy with the first version."

"This looked simple until we built it."

---

# Headlines

Headlines should create curiosity.

Not hype.

Examples

Music should feel social.

Shared playlists deserve better.

One redesign.

Hundreds of tiny decisions.

The player got out of your way.

Listening is better together.

The smallest animation mattered most.

This took five redesigns.

Realtime isn't magic.

We removed 2,000 lines of code.

---

# Captions

A good caption tells a story.

Structure

Context

↓

Problem

↓

Decision

↓

Result

↓

Question

Example

Shared playlists are everywhere.

But they often feel like shared documents instead of shared experiences.

We wanted collaboration to feel natural.

Here's how we approached it.

What would you improve?

---

# Questions

End posts with thoughtful questions.

Avoid engagement bait.

Good

What would you change?

Have you experienced this before?

Would this improve your workflow?

How would you design this?

What's the hardest part about building this?

Avoid

Comment YES.

Tag a friend.

Double tap.

Follow for more.

---

# Technical Writing

When discussing engineering:

Avoid unnecessary jargon.

Explain ideas simply.

Use diagrams when useful.

Focus on why.

Not just how.

---

# Design Writing

Talk about decisions.

Not aesthetics alone.

Instead of

"We changed the button."

Say

"We increased the spacing so the interface felt calmer."

---

# Product Writing

Talk about user problems.

Not features.

Instead of

"Added collaborative playlists."

Say

"We wanted playlists to feel like conversations."

---

# Music Writing

Music is emotional.

Technology supports the experience.

Technology is never the hero.

Music is.

---

# Things We Never Say

Revolutionary

Game-changing

Industry-leading

Best ever

Ultimate

Disruptive

Groundbreaking

Next generation

Unmatched

State-of-the-art

World class

AI-powered (unless genuinely important)

---

# Emoji Usage

Minimal.

Occasional.

Intentional.

Never decorate sentences with emojis.

---

# Hashtags

Only when useful.

Prefer quality over quantity.

---

# Storytelling Formula

Every post should answer:

Why did we build this?

What problem existed?

What changed?

Why does it matter?

What did we learn?

---

# The Swipe Principle

Each slide should create curiosity for the next.

Avoid repeating information.

Reward the reader for continuing.

---

# Writing Checklist

Before publishing ask:

Is every sentence necessary?

Could this be shorter?

Did we explain why?

Does it sound human?

Would a founder actually say this?

Does it teach something?

Does it respect the reader's time?

---

# The Final Test

If we remove the LiViL logo from this post,

someone should still recognize our voice.

That is consistency.

That is the goal.
